Who homecare may help

Many families reach a point where looking after someone at home becomes difficult. Homecare is for those moments.

If you are not sure whether a home visit would suit your relative, send an enquiry or call us. The team will ask a few questions and help you decide.

  • Older adults who need help with daily activities
  • People recovering at home after illness, surgery or hospital discharge
  • Patients who need ongoing support with daily care
  • Families caring for a sick or dependent relative
  • People who may benefit from an appropriately assessed home visit

What families can ask about

These are the kinds of support families ask about. Tell us what you need and the team will explain what NexaCare can arrange for your situation, and whether a home visit is suitable.

Personal care and nursing are different. Help with washing, dressing and getting around is personal care. Nursing and other clinical care are provided by suitably qualified staff. We will tell you which is being proposed.

Prefer to speak to a clinician remotely first? See telemedicine.

  • Elderly care and daily living

    Help with everyday activities such as washing, dressing, meals and getting around.

  • Nursing visits

    Nursing care at home, where it is suitable and appropriate for the person.

  • Recovery after illness or hospital stay

    Support while you recover at home, with follow-up agreed with the team.

  • Mobility and physiotherapy

    Help with moving safely, and physiotherapy visits where suitable.

  • Medication support

    Help to take medicines as prescribed, within professional authorisation.

  • Mother and baby visits

    Support for new mothers and babies at home, where suitable.

  • Guidance for family caregivers

    Practical advice for relatives looking after someone at home.

How homecare works

A simple path from your first call to care at home.

  1. Contact us

    Call, or send the enquiry form below.

  2. We talk it through

    The team asks about the person’s needs and your concerns.

  3. Assessment or care plan

    Where suitable, the team arranges an assessment and explains a care plan.

  4. Care is coordinated

    Confirmed visits are agreed with you, including dates and who will visit.

  5. Follow-up

    Follow-up is agreed as part of the care plan.

Why families choose care at home

  • Care in a familiar, comfortable place
  • Support for dignity and independence
  • Easier coordination between the family and the care team
  • Fewer journeys, when it is clinically suitable

Homecare does not replace hospital treatment, and not every patient is suitable for care at home. The team will advise honestly.

For the family

Looking after someone you love can be tiring and worrying. You do not have to work everything out alone.

Call us, share your concerns, and we will explain the next steps. You may find these guides helpful: preventing falls, healthy ageing and medication safety.

Homecare questions

How do I know if homecare is right for my relative?

Send an enquiry or call us. The team will ask about the person’s needs and tell you whether a home visit may be suitable.

What happens after I send the form?

A member of the team contacts you by your preferred method. Nothing is arranged until you have spoken with us.

Which areas do you visit?

Please give your location or estate in the form. The team will confirm whether a visit is possible there.

How do I ask about fees?

Please ask when the team contacts you, or call us.

Can I be involved in the care?

Yes. Families are welcome to take part, and the team can explain how to coordinate with the care team.

What if it is an emergency?

Do not wait for an enquiry to be answered. Go to the nearest emergency facility or call emergency services.

Should I include medical details in the form?

No. Keep the note brief and general. The team will ask for any further information during a conversation.
