Healthy ageing

Older adults

Overview

Healthy ageing is about staying as independent, active and connected as possible as we get older. Small, regular habits make a real difference.

Common changes with age

  • Reduced strength and balance.
  • Changes in vision and hearing.
  • Changes in sleep.
  • Slower recovery from illness.
  • Mild forgetfulness is common, but sudden or marked confusion is not a normal part of ageing.

Everyday habits that help

  • Stay active, including exercises for strength and balance.
  • Eat a varied diet and drink enough fluids.
  • Stay in touch with family, friends and community.
  • Keep long-term conditions under review and take medicines as directed.
  • Have vision and hearing checked.
  • Ask about vaccinations and screening that suit your age and health.

When to seek a health assessment

  • Unexplained weight loss or loss of appetite.
  • Increasing forgetfulness or confusion.
  • Low mood or loneliness that does not lift.
  • Falls, or fear of falling.
  • Difficulty managing daily tasks or medicines.

Get urgent medical help if you notice

  • Sudden confusion
  • Sudden weakness, a drooping face or difficulty speaking
  • Chest pain or severe breathlessness
  • A fall with a head injury, severe pain or being unable to move

Go to the nearest emergency facility or call emergency services. Do not wait for an appointment.

This page gives general health information in plain language. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace advice from a qualified health professional. This page is education only and does not mean NexaCare provides specialist care for this condition. Call us to ask what is available.
