Fall prevention for older adults

Older adults

Overview

A fall is when a person unintentionally comes to rest on the ground or a lower level. Falls are common in older adults and can cause serious injury, but many falls can be prevented.

Signs someone may be at risk

  • Unsteady walking, or holding onto furniture to get around.
  • A previous fall.
  • Fear of falling, or doing less because of it.
  • Dizziness on standing up.

Common causes

  • Muscle weakness and poor balance
  • Some medicines, or taking several at once
  • Poor lighting, loose rugs, slippery floors and stairs
  • Poor vision or unsuitable footwear
  • Dizziness, long-term conditions or a drop in blood pressure on standing

Prevention and staying well

  • Do exercises that build strength and balance, as advised by a health professional.
  • Remove trip hazards, improve lighting and add handrails and non-slip mats.
  • Wear well-fitting shoes with good grip.
  • Have vision and hearing checked.
  • Ask for a review of medicines.
  • Stand up slowly after lying or sitting.

When to seek a health assessment

  • After any fall, even if you feel unhurt.
  • Fear of falling is limiting what you do.
  • You notice new dizziness or unsteadiness.

Get urgent medical help if you notice

  • A head injury, especially with drowsiness, vomiting or confusion
  • Severe pain or being unable to move or put weight on a limb
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Heavy bleeding

Go to the nearest emergency facility or call emergency services. Do not wait for an appointment.

This page gives general health information in plain language. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace advice from a qualified health professional. This page is education only and does not mean NexaCare provides specialist care for this condition. Call us to ask what is available.
